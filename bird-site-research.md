# Bird-site research findings

The five independent site checks are complete. Facts and numerical results below retain the scope and limitations stated in the sources; they are not interchangeable with current population estimates.

## Valiyaparamba Backwaters

Kerala Tourism says “about 90 varieties of bird species” have been identified at Valiyaparamba. The page gives no survey date, protocol, observers or checklist, and the figure was not traced to an original study. The Government of India’s Incredible India page repeats a nearly-90 figure without methods. The website can report that this is an approximate figure published by Kerala Tourism, while explicitly noting that the page supplies no survey context. Do not present it as an independently verified survey total or infer species from it. [Kerala Tourism](https://www.keralatourism.org/bekal/valiyaparamba-backwaters.php) · [Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/kerala/kasargod/valiyaparamba-backwaters)

A separate district checklist reports 366 bird species for Kasaragod as a whole, using public eBird data and published literature through 29 May 2020 plus selected records confirmed with birdwatchers. This is a district-level list, not a Valiyaparamba count. [Rodrigues, “A Checklist of the Birds of Kasargod District, Kerala, India,” Malabar Trogon 18(1), 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342961116_A_Checklist_of_the_Birds_of_Kasaragod_District_Kerala_India)

## Kidoor

The article “Avian Diversity of Kidoor, a village in the lateritic midlands of Kasaragod district, North Kerala,” by Maxim Rodrigues, Prashantha Krishna, Raju Kidoor and Rayan Pradeep reports 156 species from the study area. The authors document observations from September 2016 to December 2018 with the Area Count Method, mostly morning/evening observations, additional night and other-time observations, eBird uploads, binoculars and camera, and field-guide identification. The article is listed as a 2019 paper in *Malabar Trogon* 16(3), pp. 44–59; available material does not establish peer-review status. The 156 is a survey-period result for the defined Kidoor study area, not a current district total. Named examples include Orange-breasted Green Pigeon, Lesser Sand Plover and Indian Nightjar. [Author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335751660_Avian_Diversity_of_Kidoor_a_village_in_the_lateritic_midlands_of_Kasaragod_district_North_Kerala) · [Scanned article PDF, *Malabar Trogon* 16(3)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f0/Kidoor_Bird_Village_2019_list_birds.pdf) · [Journal archive](https://malabarnhs.in/trogon.html) · [Author bibliography](https://maximrodriguesk.weebly.com/research-poster-presentations--contributed-talks.html)

The article describes the study area as lateritic plains, paddy fields, wooded areas, arecanut and coconut plantations, and wetlands including parts of the Shiriya River, at approximately 12.63°N, 74.98°E. [Author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335751660_Avian_Diversity_of_Kidoor_a_village_in_the_lateritic_midlands_of_Kasaragod_district_North_Kerala)

## Chemmattamvayal Wetlands

A *Journal of Threatened Taxa* paper by Sreehari K. Mohan, R. Anjitha and K. Maxim Rodrigues reports 145 species, across 17 orders and 50 families, from December 2014 to March 2018. It identifies 42 as winter migrants. The full Methods describe regular fixed-transect surveys, morning and evening sessions, additional opportunistic records and 15-minute checklists; the abstract also characterizes observations as mainly incidental and says eBird data were used. The paper’s abstract reports 42 winter migrants and 97 birds seen throughout the year, which sums to 139 rather than 145; do not repeat those two categories as a complete breakdown. The authors discuss the wetland and adjacent habitats as a stepping stone for migrants, and cite nearby land conversion as a threat. Example recorded species include White-throated Kingfisher, Indian Pond Heron, Spotted Dove, Garganey, Common Teal, Black-headed Ibis and Oriental Darter. Treat all totals as historical study findings. [Original article page](https://www.threatenedtaxa.org/index.php/JoTT/article/view/5246) · [Full-text PDF](https://www.threatenedtaxa.org/index.php/JoTT/article/download/5246/9219/51256) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.11609/jott.5246.15.4.23047-23060)

## Kumbla Estuary

A 2006 Zoological Survey of India account describes the Kumbla–Shiriya confluence at Kozhipady Kadappuram as extensive intertidal mudflats and mangrove, naming *Avicennia officinalis*, *Rhizophora mucronata*, *Aegiceras corniculatum*, *Excoecaria agallocha* and *Acanthus ilicifolius*. It says low-tide mudflats are foraging grounds for shorebirds. The account does not give survey dates, standardized effort or a site-wide species total, and its qualitative importance claim is not a formal protection designation. It names Green Shanks, Red Shanks, Lesser Sand Plover, Large Sand Plover and Common Sand Piper as shorebirds. A separate district checklist update reports one Great Thick-knee photographed at Kumbla-Shiriya Estuary on 27 December 2020 and observed again during the Asian Waterbird Census on 2 January 2021; this is a documented individual, not an abundance estimate. [ZSI Occasional Paper No. 246, p. 23](https://zsi.gov.in/public/PDFVolumes/occpapers/246/index.pdf) · [Kasaragod bird checklist additions through February 2023](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374510390_Checklist_of_the_birds_of_Kasaragod_district_additions_till_February_2023)

## Udinoor Polders

A 7 December 2025 *Onmanorama* report describes a Gadwall sighting in Udinoor paddy polders and attributes a total of 173 species there to eBird. Direct access to the underlying eBird checklist was blocked during research, so dates, lists and methods could not be independently checked. Attribute the count to the news report and eBird rather than presenting it as independently verified. No reliable source was found for a site area, formal wetland designation, complete seasonal list or population total. Do not transfer Chemmattamvayal survey totals to Udinoor. [Onmanorama, 7 Dec. 2025](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2025/12/07/gadwall-duck-kanhangad--asaragod-avian-diversity.html) · [Kerala State Land Use Board Wetland Information System](https://kslub.kerala.gov.in/?p=1480&lang=en)

## Regional species occurrence evidence available for profiles

The Kidoor paper supports local records of Orange-breasted Green Pigeon, Lesser Sand Plover and Indian Nightjar. The Chemmattamvayal paper supports local records of White-throated Kingfisher, Indian Pond Heron, Spotted Dove, Garganey, Common Teal, Black-headed Ibis and Oriental Darter. The ZSI Kumbla–Shiriya account supports its named shorebird examples; the 2023 update documents a Great Thick-knee individual there in 2020–21. The 2025 *Onmanorama* story reports a Gadwall at Udinoor but the underlying eBird record was inaccessible. These local records do not establish abundance, current population or occurrence in every Kasaragod habitat.


## Later district checklist updates and locality coverage (reviewed 19 November 2026)

The 2023 update by Maxim Rodrigues adds 34 species to the 366-species 2020 district checklist, for a published total of 400. The updated field guide includes these 34 additions; Great Thick-knee was already present in the earlier curated profiles and is not duplicated.

Two later district additions are included as dated news-reported records:
- Kashmir Flycatcher (*Ficedula subrubra*) at Chemmattamvayal, reported in May 2026; the report attributes a district total of 414 to eBird.
- Black-breasted Weaver (*Ploceus benghalensis*) at Cheemeni Ariyittapara, reported on 26 October 2026; the report says this raised the district total to 421.

These are dated district-level totals, not a complete downloadable 421-species list. The 2023 article is the latest fully itemized published additions list located during this review. The site therefore labels its bird cards as documented records and does not claim that the illustrated cards alone constitute the entire district checklist.

### Kolathur and Kundamkuzhy

The Bedadka Grama Panchayat profile identifies Kolathur and Kundamkuzhy among its villages/divisions, but does not provide bird records or a locality checklist. The DTPC Kasaragod description of Thonikadavu identifies it as being in Kundamkuzhy village and describes evergreen meadows, forest and watercourses; it does not name bird species. No species are assigned to Kolathur or Thonikadavu solely from district-wide records.

The 2023 district-update record of Tickell’s Leaf Warbler is specifically located at Thachiru Mala, Koliyar on 7 November 2020. The source does not establish that this is Kolathur, so the site preserves the published locality name without relabelling it.
